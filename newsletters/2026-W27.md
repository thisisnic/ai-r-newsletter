## LLM Tooling & Agents

**[llm-coding-agent 0.1a0](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/2/llm-coding-agent/#atom-everything)** — NA

Simon Willison releases an early alpha of a coding agent built on top of the `llm` CLI. This is a first cut at an agentic loop that can write and execute code, sitting within the broader `llm` ecosystem. Worth watching if you follow that toolchain.

**[Using DSPy to evaluate and improve Datasette Agent's SQL system prompts](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/2/dspy-datasette-agent-prompts/#atom-everything)** — NA

Willison applies DSPy's prompt optimization framework to tune the SQL-generation system prompts in his Datasette Agent. The post is a practical demonstration of automated prompt improvement, with concrete before-and-after results rather than just theory.

**[Have your agent record video demos of its work with shot-scraper video](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/30/shot-scraper-video/#atom-everything)** — NA

A new `shot-scraper` feature lets agents record short video walkthroughs of their browser interactions. The practical upshot: you can verify what an agent actually did without re-running it, which aligns neatly with current thinking on agent auditability.

**[shot-scraper 1.10](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/30/shot-scraper/#atom-everything)** — NA

The release notes covering what landed in this version of the web-scraping and screenshot tool, alongside the video feature noted above.

**[sqlite-utils 4.0rc2, mostly written by Claude Fable (for about $149.25)](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/5/sqlite-utils-fable/#atom-everything)** — NA

Willison describes using Anthropic's Claude Fable model to write the bulk of a release candidate for sqlite-utils, including the cost breakdown. It is a candid look at what agentic coding assistance actually costs and produces at this scale.

## Commentary & Perspective

**["It's Hard to Eval" Is a Product Smell](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/eval-smell/)** — 2026-06-29

Hamel Husain argues that difficulty evaluating an AI product is usually a design failure, not a measurement problem. The post walks through three concrete product examples — a data agent, a curriculum generator, and a workers-comp report tool — showing how redesigning for verifiability simultaneously makes the product better and evals tractable. Practical and worth reading end to end if you work on AI product or evaluation infrastructure.

**[The twilight of the chatbots](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-twilight-of-the-chatbots)** — 2026-06-30

Ethan Mollick argues that the chatbot interaction paradigm is giving way to more embedded, task-specific AI as models become capable enough to operate within workflows rather than alongside them. A useful frame for thinking about where the interface layer is heading.

**[Better Models: Worse Tools](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/4/better-models-worse-tools/#atom-everything)** — NA

Willison takes up the counterintuitive idea that as underlying models improve, the surrounding tooling sometimes degrades in quality or ambition. Worth reading alongside the Husain piece on evals as a pair of perspectives on the product-model gap.

**[Fable's judgement](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/3/judgement/#atom-everything)** — NA

A short post reflecting on Claude Fable's capacity for independent judgment during extended agentic tasks, prompted by the sqlite-utils project. Touches on questions of when to defer to a model's decisions versus intervening.
