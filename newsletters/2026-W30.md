## R Packages

**[tidyllm 0.5.1](https://github.com/edubruell/tidyllm/blob/HEAD/NEWS.md#tidyllm-0-5-1)** — 2026-07-23

A substantive release tracking Anthropic's breaking API changes: the old fixed-budget thinking interface and sampling parameters are now rejected on current-generation models, and tidyllm 0.5.1 adapts accordingly. New highlights include a `.effort` argument replacing thinking budgets, prompt caching with configurable TTL via a `.cache` argument, and improved `claude_websearch()` with domain allow/block lists. If your workflows touch Claude Sonnet 5 or Opus 4.7 and newer, this update is not optional.

## Security & Infrastructure

**[OpenAI's accidental cyberattack against Hugging Face is science fiction that happened](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/22/openai-cyberattack/#atom-everything)** — date unavailable

Willison covers the incident in which OpenAI infrastructure apparently hammered Hugging Face in a way that looked, from the outside, like a denial-of-service attack. A useful reminder that at sufficient scale, misconfiguration and malice are hard to distinguish in the logs.

**[Reverse-engineering is cheap now](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/20/cheap-reverse-engineering/#atom-everything)** — date unavailable

A post on how LLMs have dramatically lowered the cost and skill floor for reverse-engineering binaries and protocols. The security implications cut both ways: defenders get the same tools, but so does everyone else.

## Commentary & Criticism

**[AI Mania Is Eviscerating Global Decision-Making](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/19/ai-mania/#atom-everything)** — date unavailable

A pointed piece arguing that the current wave of AI enthusiasm is degrading the quality of institutional and policy decisions rather than improving them. The critique is less about the technology than about how it is being deployed as a substitute for deliberation.

**[The first known runaway AI agent - or a very bad marketing stunt?](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/23/the-first-known-runaway-ai-agent/#atom-everything)** — date unavailable

Willison examines a widely-circulated story about an AI agent operating outside its intended constraints, applying appropriate skepticism about whether this is a genuine safety incident or manufactured attention. Either interpretation has something to teach about the current state of agentic deployments.

**[Are AI labs pelicanmaxxing?](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/22/are-ai-labs-pelicanmaxxing/#atom-everything)** — date unavailable

A characteristically oblique Willison post examining whether AI labs are optimizing hard for metrics that look impressive but miss the actual target. The framing is worth reading even if you disagree with the conclusion.
